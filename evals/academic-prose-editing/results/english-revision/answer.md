Routine measurements cover common conditions. Rare events are less often observed, leaving limited evidence for decisions in those conditions. We combine routine records with targeted follow-up measurements to address this coverage gap.

The cross-site model uses paired readings from calibrated channels collected after temperature stabilization. It accepts a sample only when both channels are available, and the site is the independent unit of analysis. Under the same evaluated protocol, the full cross-site procedure achieved 33/50 successful predictions, compared with 28/50 for the fixed baseline. This comparison does not isolate the contribution of weighting because the procedures differ in both weighting and data eligibility, and no component ablation was performed.

The protocol defines the map as `f: X → Y`, the contrast as `a - b`, the mixture as `1:2`, and the operating interval as `10–15 °C`. The archived protocol is located at `https://example.org/protocol`. Its historical annotation reads “Check: retain both channels — otherwise abstain.”
