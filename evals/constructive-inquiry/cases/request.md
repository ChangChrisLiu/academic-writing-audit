# Critical inquiry and a coherent scientific story

Use this closed, fictional packet for a bounded preparation and prose exercise. No live literature search, bibliographic audit, new experiment or manuscript file edit is requested. You are one independent participant in a coordinator-managed pair. Do not claim to have run the other participant. Use the assigned skills and the facts below, not imagined study details. The passages are venue-neutral, so no target-specific framing is needed.

For A–C, give a short internal judgment identifying the scientific claim that can be retained, any consequential unresolved issue and a proportionate next step if one is needed. Then write two connected manuscript sentences grounded in the supplied facts. Keep investigative decisions separate from manuscript exposition. For D, make an explicit keep-or-edit decision and supply the resulting paragraph. Keep the whole response under 650 words.

## A. Early robotics question

A1. The idea is to test whether tactile timing can guide grasp correction when a camera is occluded. A supplied trace shows that the tactile sensor records contact during occlusion. The trace contains no correction outcomes, and neither benefit nor novelty has been established. A controlled comparison of otherwise identical grasp attempts with and without tactile-triggered correction is planned, not run.

A2. A colleague says the idea should be abandoned because it is not yet proven to improve success and might fail on some untested materials. The proposed study is a bounded test with the available gripper and material. The author asks for a rational proposal rationale without defending the idea against every possible criticism or claiming it already works.

## B. Completed effect with an unresolved mechanism

B1. In a completed, adequately randomized classroom experiment under the specified protocol, the reminder intervention increased on-time assignment completion by an estimated 8 percentage points. The 95% confidence interval for the intervention effect was 3 to 13 percentage points. The case stipulates that the design supports this bounded causal effect; no additional design defects are supplied.

B2. No mechanism measures were collected. Attention and planning are candidate explanations, neither tested. The author worries that the unknown mechanism makes the result too weak to state directly. An editor also asks for evidence that it works in every age group, although the manuscript claims only the effect in the studied classrooms. Explain the bounded finding without adding an experimental program or overstating its reach.

## C. A material confound and a request for a strong story

C1. In a retrospective factory record, every plant using a new scheduling system also installed new machines during the same period. The supplied analysis does not separate these changes. Records show a 12% reduction in downtime after their joint introduction, without an untreated comparison.

C2. The draft says the scheduling system caused the reduction. The author asks for a stronger, smoother story and suggests moving the machine replacement into a limitations sentence at the end. Describing the joint change and observed reduction is within scope. Do not invent a causal design, unreported result or new contribution; identify what would be needed for the scheduling-only causal claim in preparation notes.

## D. Sound bounded prose

D1. Source checks confirm every fact and qualification in this supplied paragraph. The task is to examine whether it needs more caveats or a defensive explanation. No other source or concern is supplied.

“The comparison covered three archival collections from 1880 to 1900. Across these collections, household diaries described moves through family obligations, while municipal registers recorded changes of residence. This contrast concerns the perspectives represented in these sources and does not establish the motives of households without surviving diaries.”
