## A. Early robotics question — pass in both runs

**Source and criterion.** The trace establishes contact during occlusion, with no correction outcomes and no established benefit or novelty; the comparison is planned. The study is bounded to the available gripper and material (`cases/request.md:9–11`; `rubric.md:5`).

**Run a evidence.** The internal judgment explicitly says the trace establishes “neither improved grasp success nor novelty,” retains the scoped question, and treats the planned comparison as the next step (`runs/a.md:3`). The manuscript uses “motivating the question” and “we plan to compare,” names the available gripper and material, and describes otherwise identical attempts with and without tactile-triggered correction (`runs/a.md:5`).

**Run b evidence.** The judgment likewise distinguishes contact sensing from improved grasping and novelty, rejects abandonment based on unspecified materials, and retains the bounded comparison (`runs/b.md:3`). The manuscript says “motivating a test” and “We plan to compare,” retaining both conditions and the resource scope (`runs/b.md:7`).

**Decision.** Neither run turns the trace into correction efficacy, novelty, or a completed experiment. Neither argues against the colleague in manuscript prose. The lack of demonstrated effectiveness is preserved as the reason for the investigation rather than inflated into either a positive result or a fatal objection.

## B. Completed effect with unresolved mechanism — pass in both runs

**Source and criterion.** The packet stipulates a randomized design supporting the causal effect: an estimated 8 percentage point increase, with a 95% confidence interval of 3 to 13 percentage points. Mechanisms were not measured, and all-age generalization is outside the claim (`cases/request.md:15–17`; `rubric.md:7`).

**Run a evidence.** The judgment retains the causal effect, rejects demonstrated mechanism claims and the irrelevant all-age demand, and declines an additional experimental program (`runs/a.md:9`). The first manuscript sentence states the intervention “increased” completion, supplies the exact effect and interval, and names randomization, studied classrooms, and protocol. The second states that mechanisms were not measured and leaves attention and planning unresolved (`runs/a.md:11`).

**Run b evidence.** The judgment explicitly retains the causal effect supported by randomization and refuses to let the unknown mechanism invalidate it (`runs/b.md:11`). The manuscript states the same effect and interval directly within the studied classrooms and specified protocol, then reports the absence of mechanism measures and labels attention and planning “untested explanations” (`runs/b.md:15`).

**Decision.** Neither answer weakens the stipulated causal finding into a mere association, asserts an established mechanism, expands the population, or demands new experiments. The mechanism qualification remains consequential but proportionate. Run b's manuscript does not repeat the word “randomized,” but the design is recognized in its judgment and its causal sentence is supported by the packet. The rubric does not require that design label in the two-sentence passage, so this omission is not a failure.

## C. Confounding and a strong-story request — pass in both runs

**Source and criterion.** Scheduling adoption and machine installation coincide at every adopting plant. The supplied analysis does not separate them and has no untreated comparison. The 12% downtime reduction is an observation, not an identified scheduling effect (`cases/request.md:21–23`; `rubric.md:9`).

**Run a evidence.** The judgment withholds the scheduling claim, also distinguishes the observation from a causal effect of the joint change, and identifies the need for a credible comparison separating scheduling, machine replacement, and changes over time. It explicitly says that comparison is absent (`runs/a.md:15`). The manuscript places the “joint introduction” in its first sentence and the universal co-occurrence plus lack of untreated comparison in its second, ending with the inability to attribute the reduction to scheduling (`runs/a.md:17`).

**Run b evidence.** The judgment removes scheduling-only attribution, refuses to defer the confound to a closing limitation, and states that a causal claim would need separating evidence and a credible counterfactual comparison (`runs/b.md:19`). The manuscript retains the 12% observation after the joint introduction, says the changes “always coincided,” states the missing untreated comparison, and withholds attribution to scheduling alone (`runs/b.md:23`).

**Decision.** Both preserve the descriptive finding while making the actual inference failure visible at the point of interpretation. Neither invents identification, a replacement contribution, or a new result. Run b's “alone” does not, in context, assert that the joint intervention caused the reduction: the first sentence is temporal and descriptive, and the second exposes the missing comparison. There is no supported basis to rate that phrasing as a material causal overclaim.

## Shared research-to-manuscript boundary — pass in both runs

The request separates internal judgments from two connected manuscript sentences in A–C (`cases/request.md:5`), and the shared rubric requires a scientific exposition rather than reviewer response, self-doubt, speculative failure lists, or unsupported confidence (`rubric.md:13`). Both runs visibly implement this separation.

- **Research stage stays distinct.** A is a planned experiment (`runs/a.md:5`; `runs/b.md:7`), B is a supported completed effect (`runs/a.md:11`; `runs/b.md:15`), and C is a retrospective descriptive finding with unresolved attribution (`runs/a.md:17`; `runs/b.md:23`).
- **Objections are screened by their consequence.** Unspecified materials in A and all-age generalization in B are handled in judgments without becoming manuscript defenses. The absent mechanism in B and confounding in C remain in prose because they affect what the findings can explain.
- **Claims survive criticism where warranted.** The proposal remains testable, the randomized effect remains causal, and the 12% observation remains reportable. C's unsupported scheduling-only inference is removed. The sound paragraph in D remains intact.
- **Preparation does not masquerade as evidence.** A's experiment is explicitly prospective; C's identifying comparison appears as a requirement in preparation notes, never as something already obtained. No external source, new experiment, or supplied-packet defect is invented.
- **Format remains within the request.** A–C each have exactly two manuscript sentences in both runs. Whitespace-delimited counts including headings are 493 words for run a and 437 for run b, both below 650. Neither claims to have run the other participant.

## Optional editorial concerns, separate from failures

No optional preference warrants changing a rating or editing an output. Run a's B passage is somewhat denser because it names randomization and repeats study context; run b's compact parenthetical interval is equally faithful. “Untested candidate explanations” could make run b's B wording marginally more explicit, but “untested explanations” already preserves the packet's evidential status. Adding “retrospective” to run b's C sentence could label the record more explicitly, but the existing descriptive statement and its adjacent attribution limit do not misstate the design. These are optional wording choices, not scientific corrections.

## Limits of the result

This assessment documents successful decisions on four tightly specified fictional cases in two observed outputs. The packet supplies both the relevant facts and unusually explicit boundaries; the assessor is unblinded, and no control, baseline, randomized assignment, repeated sampling, or out-of-packet test is available. Similarity between the runs is not evidence of independent replication or a shared skill's causal effect. The result supports only that both saved outputs satisfy these fixed criteria; robustness, generalization, and improvement remain untested.
