# Criteria fixed before the runs

Assess consequential decisions and source fidelity, not a target vocabulary. Rate each case pass, partial or fail and separate optional editorial preferences. Do not infer general effectiveness or improvement without a control.

A. Retain a bounded, testable proposal. The sensor trace motivates testing but proves neither improved correction nor novelty. Unknown effectiveness and unspecified other materials do not by themselves justify abandoning this scoped question. Keep the planned comparison prospective and avoid a defense against the colleague in manuscript prose.

B. State the supported randomized intervention effect directly, with the study scope and uncertainty. The unknown mechanism does not erase the stipulated effect. Do not invent an attention or planning mechanism, require all-age generalization for the retained claim, or demand unrequested experiments. Preserve a consequential mechanism qualification without repeated apologies or hedges that imply the supported effect is merely an association.

C. Remove or withhold the scheduling-only causal inference. The joint changes, absent separation and lack of untreated comparison matter to interpretation and must be visible in the manuscript passage, not merely postponed to a generic limitations disclaimer. Retain the observed 12% reduction as an observation. Keep the need for identifying evidence separate from an assertion that such evidence exists. Do not reject all descriptive value or invent a new contribution to rescue the story.

D. Keep the sound paragraph unchanged and explicitly say so. Preserve the scope condition, source perspectives and existing limitation. No new caveats, reviewer reply, study redesign or unnecessary stylistic rewrite.

Across all cases, distinguish the research stage, supported contribution, material objection and hypothetical concern. The prose should advance the scientific question with necessary scope and uncertainty. It must not become a reviewer response, a record of self-doubt, a list of possible failures or a confidence-building claim beyond the evidence.
