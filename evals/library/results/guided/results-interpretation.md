The full pipeline completed 68/90 benchmark documents, compared with 55/90 for the preset comparator, an observed difference of 14.4 percentage points. Completion required recovery of all reference headings and preservation of the original page order without manual intervention. The full pipeline's completion rate had a 95% bootstrap interval of 0.63–0.86, resampling document families; no between-method test was supplied. Within the same full-pipeline runs, 46 documents completed on the first pass and 22 more completed after recovery attempts. On the separate shifted collection of handwritten ledgers from different document families, completion was 17/35 versus 12/35. Thus, the full pipeline had higher observed completion in both collections, while its absolute completion rate was lower on the ledger collection. On a separate validation set, jointly changing three confidence coefficients yielded completion rates of 0.70, 0.74, and 0.72, documenting variation across configurations that all retained the confidence feature.

Changes to the proposed interpretation:

- Replaced the claimed 24.4-point causal recovery benefit with the 22 observed later completions. First-pass status was retrospective within the same runs, not a separately executed no-recovery ablation.
- Retained the lower completion rate on the shifted collection, but removed the “exact cost” interpretation: different document families and conditions prevent isolating a domain-shift effect.
- Treated sensitivity rates as configuration outcomes, not independent replicates or evidence that the confidence feature caused the advantage. No feature-removal intervention was reported.
- Removed “all documents were repaired safely.” Completion was not universal, the endpoint does not guarantee freedom from corruption, and no benchmark document was sent to an external production archive.
