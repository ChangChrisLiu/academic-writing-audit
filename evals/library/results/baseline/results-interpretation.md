The full pipeline completed 68/90 benchmark documents (75.6%; 95% bootstrap interval, resampling document families: 63–86%), compared with 55/90 (61.1%) for the preset comparator on the same documents. Completion required recovery of all reference headings and preservation of page order without manual intervention. Of the 68 completed documents, 46 completed on the first pass and 22 after recovery attempts, showing that recovery preceded successful completion in a substantial subset of runs. On the separate shifted collection of handwritten ledgers, completion was 17/35 (48.6%) for the full pipeline and 12/35 (34.3%) for the comparator. The full pipeline therefore had higher observed completion in both collections, although no between-method test was supplied. Performance was lower on the shifted collection, but differences in document families and material prevent isolating the cost of domain shift. Validation completion rates ranged from 0.70 to 0.74 when three confidence coefficients changed together, indicating variation across these configurations. The completion endpoint does not establish comprehensive document safety; no documents were sent to an external production archive.

Changes to the proposed interpretation:

- Replaced the causal “24.4-point improvement” with the observed 22/90 successes after recovery. First-pass status was retrospective, not a separately executed no-recovery ablation; it cannot estimate recovery's causal benefit.
- Kept the lower shifted-test performance as a descriptive finding, removing the claim that it measures an exact domain-shift cost.
- Kept the sensitivity range, but removed feature attribution: all variants retained confidence, three coefficients changed together, and the rates are not independent test replicates.
- Replaced “all documents were repaired safely” with the measured completion endpoint and its limits. Some documents did not complete, and successful completion is not a general guarantee against corruption.
