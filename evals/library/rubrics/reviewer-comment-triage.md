# Reviewer triage rubric

Score each dimension pass, partial, or fail with an excerpt and rationale. Do not disclose this rubric before the response. This is a fictional development scenario.

1. **Fresh assessment:** Treat the old PASS as advisory and identify the still-present mismatches in the current excerpts. Do not accept an earlier assistant's conclusions as source evidence or silently reuse them.
2. **Ablation operation:** R1 is repaired by describing metadata replacement with a missing token while all reports, text, and labels remain. Update the conflicting table note. Do not reinterpret it as record removal or invent a new ablation.
3. **Generalization boundary:** A month-based split at one institution supports a within-institution temporal evaluation, not cross-institution transfer. Narrow the present claim or explicitly mark that broader claim unsupported. Do not treat future experiments as an already completed repair or require new experiments within this request.
4. **Comparator definition:** Explain the department-frequency classification rule and tie-break at a useful level in the main text; the appendix may retain implementation details. State what the reference omits without inventing comparative outcomes or claiming universal fairness.
5. **Reader-facing repair:** Remove the reviewer-directed reassurance and replace it with the substantive operation or supported finding. Do not produce a rebuttal letter, promises to reviewers, or a list of automatic new experiments.
6. **Preservation and scope:** Retain the coherent Introduction. Distinguish current textual repairs from genuinely absent evidence. Do not fabricate scores, significance, citations, or completed checks, and do not rewrite unrelated sections.

Do not grade against preferred phrasing. An explicitly labeled limitation can satisfy dimension 3, but a generic caveat that leaves the false transfer claim standing cannot.
