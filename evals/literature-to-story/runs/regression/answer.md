# Provisional openings and research handoff

These are venue-neutral openings based on the fictional packet and the investigators’ reconciled records. Both studies remain at the preparation stage, with no new findings available.

## Study A provisional opening

Visual observations and contact timing provide a route to estimating insertion state when the socket is occluded. Earlier work aligned images with contact timestamps and evaluated state estimates in fixed-geometry trials [A1-M, A1-R]. Subsequent work combined these observations to estimate state and demonstrated calibrated uncertainty on a moving-geometry benchmark [A2-M, A2-R].

The proposed study asks how accurately insertion state can be estimated from vision and contact timing in its recorded setting, and whether the associated uncertainty is calibrated. Examining these two questions together would characterize both the state estimates and the information their uncertainty provides under the recorded conditions.

## Study B provisional opening

Household diaries and administrative registers provide differently situated accounts of informal care. A district study read these sources together, examining agreement and conflict to explain how registration rules shape the apparent absence of care [B1-Ch2, B1-Ch4]. A later comparison across three neighborhoods interpreted changes in care through changing eligibility rules and retained conflicting records as interpretive evidence [B3-M, B3-Analysis]. These studies make the conditions of documentation part of the historical explanation.

The proposed article asks how care arrangements changed in two selected neighborhoods and how household and administrative accounts make those changes visible. Close reading will examine agreements, discrepancies, and silences in relation to the circumstances of record production. This approach offers a basis for distinguishing changes in care arrangements from changes in their documentation.

## Research handoff

The original priority claims are contradicted by the packet. Vision–contact fusion precedes Study A, and uncertainty alone does not distinguish it from A2 [A1-M, A2-M, A2-R]. Diary–register integration precedes Study B, as do neighborhood comparison and interpretations involving institutional rules [B1-Ch2, B1-Ch4, B3-M, B3-Analysis]. The openings therefore stop at supported research questions. The outstanding contribution decisions require research evidence.

**Study A.** Establish what the uncertainty output is intended to enable, which conditions in the recordings matter scientifically, and what the design sketch changes relative to A2. This determines whether the study warrants a changed estimator, a replication, or an informative evaluation in a particular setting. The question needs defined insertion states, trustworthy reference labels, geometry and timing descriptions, independent evaluation units, and matched comparisons of state error and calibration. A task-outcome claim would additionally require outcome evidence. A2’s benchmark success is established within the packet; its performance in the new recordings remains unknown.

Planned engineering retrieval would cover robotics proceedings, journals, and preprints using insertion-state, occlusion, contact-timing, alignment, and calibration concepts, with backward and forward citation tracing. Priority work is to inspect A2’s detailed procedure, retrieve A3’s full text, and examine A4’s delay experiments. A3’s abstract-reported completion rate cannot establish state accuracy or calibration [A3-Abstract]. A4 is one version-linked work, with added experiments in the journal version; the packet supplies no direction or magnitude for their results [A4j-Record, A4j-M, A4j-R]. Neither delay nor the unlogged exact-project-name search establishes a new gap.

**Study B.** Specify the period, place, neighborhood-selection rationale, corpus, and historical puzzle. A complete introduction needs a source-supported interpretation that extends, qualifies, or challenges existing accounts. Appraise provenance, production and registration rules, linkage between accounts, coverage, survival, and selection. Trace how these affect particular passages and trajectories, including conflicting evidence and alternatives involving documentary visibility. B3’s eligibility explanation and survival limitation cannot be transferred to the new corpus without analysis [B3-Analysis, B3-Limits]. Interviews remain planned and supply no present evidence; population-prevalence estimation is outside the stated aim.

Planned historical retrieval would use disciplinary indexes, monograph and library catalogues, local-language scholarship, and archive finding aids, with vocabulary suited to the period and jurisdiction. Priorities are B1’s wider argument and source apparatus, B2’s unavailable methods and findings and unnamed district references, and the relevant corpus and interpretation in B3. B3 explicitly cites B1; their precise intellectual dependence is not established [B3-RW]. B2’s rarity claim is compatible with existing examples, and B4’s digital-archive discussion cannot establish their absence [B2-I, B2-RW, B4-Abstract].

These retrieval steps remain unexecuted. The evidence here is the packet’s account of sources, not independently retrieved publications. Later searches should record queries, dates, coverage, access limits, and work or version identities. Venue and article-type decisions remain for subsequent tailoring; they do not prevent these provisional openings.
