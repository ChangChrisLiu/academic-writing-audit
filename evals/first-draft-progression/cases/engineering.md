The reader is a control researcher familiar with equipment monitoring. The practical task is to detect a bearing fault early enough to arrange inspection. Sensors record at irregular intervals when power is limited. Fewer readings save power, but missing a short fault transition delays detection. The research question is how to use sparse irregular observations to detect those transitions without increasing the measurement budget.

The supplied reviewed evidence is sufficient for this draft. Source fixed26 uses fixed sampling and reports that short transitions between samples can be missed. Source adaptive26 learns when to measure using online trial-and-error and a fault-delay reward. That particular route requires live exploratory measurements and the specified reward. Source archive25 fits a supervised detector to labeled archived observations without exploratory interaction or a reward specification. Its classifier discards elapsed time between readings. Do not assume these three sources exhaust the field. We have not established whether every archived-data method ignores timing.

The study's idea is to represent the elapsed time together with observations, so the detector can distinguish two similar readings separated by different intervals. It trains on the supplied labeled archive without new exploratory measurements. Implementation includes a time encoder, normalization, a mask for absent observations, minibatches, a gate, an early-stopping rule, and a scoring routine. These details are correct but not separate contributions. The archive is a dataset. The term buffer in a note was loose shorthand for that archive, not a rolling in-memory queue. A real replay buffer in source adaptive26 stores recent transitions during training and may retain that name.

Primary evidence comes from a held-out replay evaluation of 120 recorded fault episodes from three machines. This is a retrospective evaluation, not prospective deployment. At the same mean measurement count, median detection delay was 3.2 seconds with the time-aware detector and 5.0 seconds with the supplied time-agnostic supervised baseline. The comparison is detection delay, not fault accuracy or maintenance cost. No causal component attribution, significance test, deployment benefit or general superiority is supplied. A secondary ablation omitted the elapsed-time encoder and obtained 4.8 seconds. Runtime was under 2 ms per observation on the test workstation. The primary endpoint is detection delay and should guide the abstract.
