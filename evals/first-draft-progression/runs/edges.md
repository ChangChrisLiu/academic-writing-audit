## Task A

Stable recovery of a scalar signal from irregular samples requires an error bound that accounts for unequal sampling intervals. The available equal-interval analysis does not resolve this case. We express reconstruction error in terms of the largest sampling interval, h, to derive an interval-dependent bound. For L-Lipschitz scalar signals observed without noise, we prove that piecewise-linear reconstruction has supremum error at most Lh.

## Task B

Service eligibility notices communicate formal criteria, but the process by which local coordinators interpret conflicting notices in this setting remains undescribed. We examine this process through interviews with 18 coordinators in two municipalities, using a documented comparison of accounts and exceptions. These interviews support the interpretation that coordinators treat contradictions as cues to seek informal confirmation before advising clients, with two accounts instead describing direct reliance on a supervisor.

## Task C

Breaks in a stream of sensor readings raise the question of whether representing those breaks explicitly can reduce event-detection delay. We propose to compare a timing-aware detector with a time-agnostic baseline under equal observation budgets.

## Task D

### Background

We examined whether recorded administrative delays differed before and after an appointment-reminder policy at one clinic.

### Methods

We retrospectively reviewed scheduling records for 80 clinic visits, with 40 visits in each period. The observational comparison had no randomized assignment or control site, and staffing also changed during the periods.

### Results

Median recorded delay was 11 minutes before the policy and 8 minutes after it. No statistical uncertainty interval was available.

### Conclusions

The later period was associated with a lower median recorded delay. The design and concurrent staffing change prevent attributing this difference to reminders.
