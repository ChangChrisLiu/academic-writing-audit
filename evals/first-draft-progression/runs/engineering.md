\begin{abstract}
Early bearing-fault detection is needed to arrange inspection, but limited power restricts sensors to sparse, irregular readings that can miss short fault transitions. The challenge is to reduce detection delay using these observations without increasing the measurement budget. We develop a supervised detector that represents elapsed time alongside observations, allowing similar readings separated by different intervals to be distinguished, and train it on a labeled archival dataset without new exploratory measurements. In a retrospective held-out replay evaluation of 120 recorded fault episodes from three machines, median detection delay was 3.2~s with the time-aware detector and 5.0~s with the time-agnostic supervised baseline at the same mean measurement count.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Bearing-fault monitoring must detect a developing fault early enough to arrange inspection. When power is limited, reducing the number of sensor readings saves energy but increases the risk of missing a short fault transition. Fixed sampling has been shown to miss transitions that occur between measurements~\cite{fixed26}. With sparse, irregular observations, the detection problem is therefore to use the available readings effectively enough to limit delay without increasing the measurement budget.

Learning when to measure offers one way to address this problem. The approach in~\cite{adaptive26} learns measurement timing through online trial-and-error using a fault-delay reward, requiring live exploratory measurements and that reward specification. Supervised learning from labeled archived observations provides a route that does not require either, as demonstrated by~\cite{archive25}. Its classifier, however, discards the elapsed time between readings. Retaining this information would let a detector distinguish similar observations separated by different intervals, motivating a study of whether timing can improve detection from an existing archive.

We develop a time-aware supervised detector that combines elapsed time with observations and trains on the labeled archival dataset. We assess detection delay in a retrospective held-out replay evaluation covering 120 recorded fault episodes from three machines. At the same mean measurement count, the detector achieved a median delay of 3.2~s, compared with 5.0~s for the time-agnostic supervised baseline.
