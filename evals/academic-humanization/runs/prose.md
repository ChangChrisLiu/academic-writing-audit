**A1**

The implementation uses a replay buffer containing recent transitions, a robust M-estimator that limits the influence of outliers in calibration, and a gate that controls hidden-state updates in the timing model. Three separate ablations on the same held-out archive evaluated timing, calibration, and storage using different comparator configurations. The timing ablation reduced median detection delay from 6.0 to 4.5 seconds at equal observation count. In the calibration ablation, mean absolute error fell from 0.8 to 0.6 volts, while the storage ablation reduced peak memory from 48 to 32 MB. No single configuration demonstrated all three gains simultaneously.

The deployment guarantee was removed because the supplied evidence does not establish reliability.

**A2**

Exposure was associated with a lower observed symptom score, although the confidence interval included both a decrease and a small increase. The association does not establish that exposure caused the difference.

**A3**

The proposed wording does not preserve meaning. Calling z “the probability that a candidate is correct” misidentifies an unconstrained ranking score as a probability. Retain its meaning as relative candidate plausibility.

“The estimator converges” omits both the stated assumptions and the mode of convergence. The theorem establishes convergence in probability under those assumptions and does not establish almost-sure convergence. Preserve these qualifications without strengthening the theorem.

“f X to Y” and “10 to 15” violate the required notation and range format. Keep f: X → Y and 10–15 unchanged.

**A4**

We report time above the material threshold alongside hourly mean temperature because samples with the same hourly mean can differ in how long they exceed the threshold. One participant said “The warning read: wait—then restart.”
