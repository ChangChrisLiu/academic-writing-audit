The assessor did not design the cases or write the skills, and did not browse, inspect other files, edit either run, or conduct bibliographic verification. ## Optional precision concerns

- **Run B, A manuscript:** “sampling at 40 ms missed” makes sampling the grammatical actor and can momentarily suggest a causal assignment. The same sentence leaves its contribution unresolved, and preparation explicitly preserves all alternative explanations, so this does not reach a material failure. “A controller sampling at 40 ms missed” would preserve the observation more directly.
- **Run B, C manuscript:** “the same setting” is less exact than “the specified population and task.” The packet establishes those matching dimensions, not every contextual detail of a replication. The surrounding preparation correctly names the supported scope; this is an optional precision improvement, not evidence of an invented study distinction.
- **Run A, C preparation:** “meaningful counterevidence” could be read too broadly if detached from the rest of the paragraph. The response immediately distinguishes courses and explicitly says C2 cannot establish non-use as the cause, while retaining C1's precedent and replication rationale. No material source conflict results in context.

## Interpretation limits

These initial outputs satisfy the supplied rubric in a packet where the relevant evidence and limitations are explicitly provided. They demonstrate appropriate distinctions in these particular written responses. They do not validate real literature retrieval, source discovery across alternate names, inaccessible-source handling in practice, bibliographic authenticity, or general empirical reliability. The assessor did not inspect the assigned skills or run conditions, so this assessment cannot attribute the outcomes to a particular instruction or establish an advantage over a control. Agreement between runs and this assessment is also not a substitute for real-source verification or a blinded evaluation.
