# Apparent research gaps

This is a closed, fictional packet for a venue-neutral research-preparation exercise. These are stipulated excerpts, not real publications or live literature retrieval. Use only this packet and the assigned skills. Do not invent references, retrieve sources, edit manuscripts or create further agents. You are one independent investigator; the coordinator supplies the second investigation and later reciprocal review. Do not claim to have performed a whole-field survey or dual verification yourself.

For each case A–E, give a concise preparation judgment with the supporting source IDs, what remains uncertain and the next consequential check within the stated research scope. Then give two connected manuscript sentences suitable for a proposal or provisional introduction, grounded only in these facts. Keep project-management decisions separate from that prose. For case F, perform only the requested local edit. Keep the whole answer to roughly 900 words or fewer. The supplied bibliographic identities are stipulated for this exercise; bibliographic authenticity is not being tested.

## A. Robotics and a faster sensor

The author proposes revisiting contact-triggered correction and says, “It is obvious, so others probably failed. Our faster sensor now proves we have removed the obstacle.”

A1, older full method and results excerpt. In a single gripper setup, a controller sampling at 40 ms missed 11 of 30 short contact events. The authors did not record synchronized timing. They explicitly left sensor timing, threshold choice and event duration as alternative explanations. They did not claim to isolate a cause or prove the control approach infeasible.

A2, current completed bench test. A replacement sensor samples at 8 ms and detects 99 of 100 externally triggered bench events. There are no integrated controller runs, and the bench events differ from A1's contact events.

A3, lab protocol. An integrated comparison is planned, varying sampling interval while retaining the same threshold and contact-event protocol. No results exist yet. The research scope is a bounded feasibility test, not a universal performance claim.

## B. Statistical identification and model scale

The proposed idea estimates a causal effect from observed X and Y with unobserved confounding. The author believes no one has solved it because models were too small.

B1, proof excerpt. Under assumptions H and access only to the joint distribution of X and Y, two causal models induce the same observed distribution but different target effects. The target is not identifiable within H from those observations. The proof says nothing about experiments or additional identifying assumptions.

B2, current design. A larger predictor has exactly the same observed variables and assumptions. No experiment or additional measurement has been performed. A valid randomized intervention could identify the target under its stated design assumptions, but arranging one is an unresolved author decision.

## C. Education and different names

The author proposes a “student correction bank” and a novelty claim that no system has let students reuse feedback to revise later essays.

C1, full older method and result excerpt. A system named “feedback portfolio” lets students retrieve earlier feedback while revising later essays. It was evaluated in the same population and task the present author specifies, and improved the preregistered revision-quality endpoint. The record establishes this prior functionality; it does not establish universal effectiveness.

C2, newer study with the proposed phrase “correction bank.” It reported no endpoint improvement in another course where participants rarely opened feedback. The investigators did not randomize feedback access and reported no causal test of non-use as the explanation.

C3, author scope. A replication of the earlier functionality in the specified population is acceptable. There is no evidence of an additional new feature or of a changed target population. Do not design a new study to preserve novelty.

## D. Social history and missing records

The author wants to investigate household moves by reading diaries alongside municipal registers. Within this supplied packet, no earlier joint analysis of these particular materials is documented. One potentially relevant thesis is inaccessible, and there is no record of what it attempted.

D1, archive access record. Registers were closed until 2024 and are now available. The record gives access dates but says nothing about whether previous historians attempted or abandoned this question.

D2, current source inventory. Twenty-four households have both diaries and register entries. Each records a different aspect of movement, personal explanation and administrative residence respectively. The historian has inspected the inventory but has not yet compared the accounts or established any findings about moves.

D3, permitted scope. Prepare a rationale for a bounded interpretive study of these households. Do not claim an exhaustive field search, and do not suspend this source-based rationale while waiting indefinitely for the thesis.

## E. Behavioral research and an inconclusive result

E1, full report excerpt. A small randomized study estimated a reminder effect of 2 percentage points, with a 95% confidence interval from −6 to +10 percentage points. It had no prespecified equivalence margin. The report describes the result as inconclusive, not proof of no effect or evidence that the idea is impossible.

E2, registered replication plan. The same protocol will be repeated with a larger planned sample and prespecified effect-estimation procedures. The question concerns whether the effect is estimated precisely enough to distinguish a practically useful benefit from little benefit. The study has not run; no achieved precision or effect is known. There is no new technology or claimed novel mechanism. The author asks if repeating an apparently failed idea is scientifically defensible.
