# Packet 2 — Research on peer review

Draft rebuttal

R1.1 Assignment and outcome
The participants were 170 peer reviewers, randomly assigned to a checklist or standard instructions, with 85 per condition. Each assessed the same deliberately flawed abstract; the outcome was identification of the planted reporting omission. Here, “reviewers” denotes study participants, distinct from reviewers evaluating this submission. The existing Methods sentence accurately describes random assignment and should remain unchanged.

R2.1 Scope of inference
This outcome does not establish improved publication quality or performance in complete manuscript review. Neither those outcomes nor editorial decisions were measured. We propose replacing the publication-quality claim with the observed finding and its scope.

R2.2 Counts
Fifty-one of 85 checklist reviewers and 44 of 85 standard-instruction reviewers identified the omission. These are descriptive counts, without a claim of statistical significance. The proposed Results replacement includes both counts. No manuscript edits have yet occurred.

Proposed Results replacement

Among peer reviewers randomly assigned to assess the same deliberately flawed abstract, 51 of 85 reviewers in the checklist condition identified the planted reporting omission, compared with 44 of 85 reviewers given standard instructions. The checklist condition therefore had a higher observed count of reviewers identifying this omission. This outcome concerns detection of one reporting omission in an abstract. Full manuscript review, editorial decisions, and publication quality were not measured.
