## Request 1 — Conference introduction

Estimating the onset of short irrigation interruptions requires observations around the transition. Routine readings track gradual changes but can leave observations widely spaced around short interruptions under the reported sampling schedule \cite{routine2024}. Event-triggered readings concentrate measurements around rapid changes, although their observation times are irregular \cite{event2025}. Integrating these records without preserving their times can obscure which readings precede or follow an interruption.

We propose an estimator that aligns routine and event-triggered records by their recorded observation times before estimating interruption onset. This integration retains background context from routine readings and denser transition observations from event readings. ## Request 1 — Journal introduction

Intermittent observations complicate the identification of when a short irrigation interruption begins. Routine readings describe gradual changes but leave widely spaced observations around short interruptions under the sampling schedule reported in \cite{routine2024}. Event-triggered readings provide denser observations around rapid changes at irregular times \cite{event2025}. These records offer complementary information, but combining them without preserving their observation times can conceal whether a reading precedes or follows an interruption. That ambiguity matters because interruption onset must be inferred from the temporal relationship among observations.

Aligning the records by their recorded observation times preserves that relationship while bringing routine background context together with denser transition observations. We implement this integration in an estimator of interruption onset. Across 32 held-out interruption events under the evaluated protocol, the estimator achieved a median onset-time error 12% lower than the routine-only estimator.
