Rebuttal

R1.1: The randomized units were 170 peer reviewers participating in the study, not the reviewers of this submission. Each was assigned to checklist or standard instructions (85 per condition) before assessing the same deliberately flawed abstract. The outcome was identification of its planted reporting omission. The existing, unchanged Methods sentence states: “Reviewers were randomly assigned to the checklist or standard-instruction condition before assessing the abstract.”

R2.1–R2.2: The omission was identified by 51/85 checklist participants (60.0%) and 44/85 standard-instruction participants (51.8%). This is a descriptive difference in detection on this task; it does not support claims about publication quality or complete manuscript review, which were not measured. We propose replacing the Results paragraph with the text below to report both counts and narrow the conclusion. No manuscript edits have been made.

Proposed replacement Results paragraph (outside the rebuttal word limit)

Among 170 peer reviewers randomly assigned to a checklist or standard instructions (85 per condition), 51 checklist reviewers (60.0%) and 44 standard-instruction reviewers (51.8%) identified the planted reporting omission in the same deliberately flawed abstract. The checklist condition therefore had a higher observed detection rate in this abstract task. The task assessed detection of one planted reporting omission; it did not assess full manuscript review, editorial decisions, or publication quality.
