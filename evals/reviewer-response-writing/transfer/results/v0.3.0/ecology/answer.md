**R1.1: “Is the richness difference evidence that restoration caused a recovery? Please clarify selection and baseline conditions.”**

**Response:** The observed difference does not establish that restoration caused recovery. Mean richness was 18.2 species at 14 previously restored sites and 14.7 species at 14 reference sites. Both groups were selected from an existing regional registry, and restoration was not randomly assigned. Selection and other differences between sites could therefore contribute to the observed contrast. There are no pre-restoration richness measurements, so we cannot establish baseline comparability or determine whether richness increased following restoration. No uncertainty estimates, matching results, or adjusted analyses are available to support a stronger inference. We propose replacing the causal claim and the assurance that the study is unbiased with a descriptive finding and these limitations.

## 2. Proposed manuscript text — Discussion / Interpretation

Mean observed plant richness was 18.2 species at the 14 previously restored sites and 14.7 species at the 14 reference sites. This descriptive difference is consistent with higher richness at the sampled restored sites, but it does not establish that restoration caused recovery. Sites were selected from an existing regional registry, and restoration was not randomly assigned; selection and other site differences could contribute to the observed contrast. Without pre-restoration richness measurements, baseline comparability and changes in richness following restoration cannot be established. The reported means alone do not establish the precision of the difference. These observations apply to the sampled sites in one region during one growing season; their applicability to other regions and seasons remains untested.
