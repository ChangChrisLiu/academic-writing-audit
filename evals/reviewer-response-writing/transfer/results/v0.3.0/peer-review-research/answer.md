## Rebuttal

**R1.1 — Design and terminology.** “Reviewers” denotes the 170 research participants, not the reviewers of this submission. Participants were randomly assigned to a checklist or standard instructions (85 per condition), then assessed the same deliberately flawed abstract. The measured outcome was identification of the planted reporting omission. The existing Methods sentence describes this assignment and remains unchanged.

**R2.1–R2.2 — Counts and scope.** Detection was observed in 51/85 checklist participants (60.0%) and 44/85 standard-instruction participants (51.8%). No uncertainty analysis is supplied, so we make no claim of statistical significance. This outcome does not establish improved publication quality or performance in complete manuscript review. Neither full manuscript review, editorial decisions, nor publication quality was measured. We propose replacing the Results paragraph with the text below; no manuscript edits have yet been made.

## Proposed replacement Results paragraph (outside the rebuttal limit)

Among the 170 peer reviewers participating in the study, 51 of 85 assigned to the checklist (60.0%) and 44 of 85 assigned to standard instructions (51.8%) identified the planted reporting omission in the same deliberately flawed abstract. The checklist condition had a higher observed detection rate, with a difference of 8.2 percentage points. No uncertainty analysis is available for this comparison. The outcome measures detection of one reporting omission in an abstract; complete manuscript review, editorial decisions, and publication quality were not evaluated.
