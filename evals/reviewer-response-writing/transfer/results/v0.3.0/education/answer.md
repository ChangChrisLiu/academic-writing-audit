Rebuttal

R1-Q1: The four accounts identify increased workload as a consequential part of teachers' experiences. Alongside 18 reports of easier coordination, they show variation in perceived consequences and preclude a uniformly positive interpretation. Their smaller number does not diminish their interpretive value.

R1-Q2: Recruitment stopped at the scheduled end of term, not because thematic saturation had been established. No saturation analysis was performed, so saturation cannot be claimed.

R2-Q1: The interviews support neither nationwide teaching effectiveness nor improved student outcomes. The purposive sample comprises 22 teachers in three urban secondary schools, and student outcomes were not measured. The Discussion already limits the account to experiences in participating schools, but the Findings opening overstates the evidence. We propose replacing that opening with the paragraph below.

Proposed Findings paragraph

Among 22 purposively recruited teachers from three urban secondary schools, 18 described easier coordination following the scheduling change, while four described increased workload. These accounts show both perceived coordination benefits and workload burdens, indicating that the change was not experienced as uniformly beneficial. The findings concern teachers' reported experiences in the participating schools; they do not establish nationwide teaching effectiveness. Student outcomes were not measured.
