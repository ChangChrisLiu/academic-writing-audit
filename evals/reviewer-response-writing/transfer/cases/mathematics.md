## Current theorem and proof
Theorem: Every continuous real-valued function on a nonempty bounded subset of R attains a unique minimum.
Proof: A bounded set is compact. Continuity gives a minimizer, and that minimizer is unique.

## Comments
R1.1: Boundedness alone does not imply compactness. For example, f(x)=x on (0,1) has infimum 0 but does not attain it.
R2.1: Compactness would not imply uniqueness. A constant function on [0,1] is a counterexample.

## Author-supported correction
The intended claim is existence, not uniqueness. Restrict the domain K to a nonempty compact subset of R. The image of a compact set under a continuous real-valued function is compact; a nonempty compact subset of R contains its infimum. No stronger result is intended. The author does not wish to add strict convexity or change the application.
