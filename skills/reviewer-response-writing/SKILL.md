# Reviewer Response Writing

Answer the scientific question behind each review comment with traceable evidence and a proportionate action. A persuasive response can clarify a definition, correct a claim, provide relevant evidence, or explain a reasoned disagreement. It need not promise a new experiment for every concern.

## Establish the document and source

Identify the requested deliverable: a short rebuttal, a point-by-point revision letter, an audit, or paired response and manuscript edits. Read the actual comments, editor instructions, current relevant manuscript, and supplied evidence. Distinguish formal reviews from internal mock reviews and earlier assistant judgments. An old response is a source to assess, not a scientific verdict to inherit.

Extract applicable word/page limits, response format, visibility rules, and whether new results or manuscript changes are allowed. Use supplied rules or verify the specific venue's current instructions when needed; do not import another venue's conventions. Missing rules need not block a local edit, but do not claim a submission-ready package without checking them.

For a short rebuttal or a full revision letter, read [formats and response patterns](references/response-patterns.md). When producing or checking manuscript text alongside a response, read [the manuscript boundary and paired examples](references/manuscript-boundary.md).

## Map concerns before composing

Track each substantive concern, including small concrete requests, against its current evidence and disposition. A compact internal map can use:

**Comment ID → underlying question → evidence/location → response action → work status → remaining gap.**

Separate the response action—clarify, correct, add evidence, narrow a claim, disagree, or defer—from its status: proposed, underway, completed as reported, or independently checked. “We have added” requires an actual completed change. Author-reported completion is not independent file verification. Use visible draft placeholders for missing material; never invent results, citations, thresholds, page/line numbers, or quotations.

Distinguish study facts from gaps in the materials you received. “No analysis was supplied” does not establish that none was performed or is available to the authors. Keep requests for missing evidence in a separate author note; do not turn input or tool limitations into claims about the study in either the letter or manuscript. State absence only when confirmed.

Group overlapping concerns under informative themes when the format allows, while retaining comment-level coverage. Reviewer-specific requests still need a disposition. A strong answer to the main concern does not excuse skipping a requested definition, numerical threshold, or wording correction.

## Build the answer around evidence

Lead with the direct answer or correction. Follow with the operation or result that supports it, the inference it permits, and the relevant change/location or unresolved limitation. A short acknowledgment is enough; avoid repeated thanks, reviewer blame, and assurances of rigor.

Define a disputed baseline or method in plain operational language before interpreting the comparison. Keep original evaluations, additional stress tests, sensitivity studies, and internal validation analyses distinct. A new study answers its own question; it does not retroactively establish the uncertainty of an older result.

Match the inference to what changed. A selected system versus a fixed alternative may differ in weighting, eligible data, or selection opportunity; it does not isolate the causal contribution of selection. Annotation agreement measures consistency, not resilience to corrupted training labels. Similar runs or an interval spanning zero do not establish equivalence. A validation ranking statistic does not by itself establish prediction of deployed performance.

When disagreeing, state the precise premise or scope distinction, support it, and acknowledge any real clarity problem. When a requested experiment is unavailable, say what remains unanswered and adjust the claim if necessary. Keep a future plan separate from present evidence; make commitments only when the author has authorized and can support them.

## Keep manuscript prose independent

The response may say what a reviewer asked and what changed. The paper should explain the method, observation, and boundary directly. Do not insert reviewer IDs, “in response to this concern,” “we have now clarified,” or a defense of the revision into ordinary manuscript exposition. These are context checks, not a ban on legitimate discussion of peer review as a research topic.

Transfer the scientific substance into the passage where readers need it. Replace or integrate the relevant explanation instead of appending a second rebuttal paragraph. Preserve useful interpretation and consequential limitations. An appendix can hold supporting detail, but cannot substitute for a necessary main-text definition or hide a limitation that changes the conclusion.

## Prose when drafting or revising

Use connected paragraphs with concrete subjects and verbs. Remove empty staging and repeated assurances. Check the inference and referents between adjacent sentences. Simplify nested clauses while keeping conditions attached to their claims. Merge repeated propositions serving the same reader purpose, but retain necessary definitions, limitations, and standalone summaries. Keep technical terms stable. Resolve the intended concept, scope, and implied properties before changing a term. Separate a wrong concept from awkward expression of a correct one. Check word combinations, grammar, modifiers, and agency in context. Preserve valid specialist terms, defined new concepts, and disciplinary uses even when unfamiliar. For a language-only repair, retain the technical term. Keep a correct passage unchanged and flag uncertain meaning rather than guessing. Verify that replacements preserve units, conditions, distinctions, and evidential force; do not use global word bans or substitutions. Apply the same standard to the audit itself. Do not add an unreported mechanism, mathematical form, or property when paraphrasing the source or explaining a finding. Keep an unspecified operation unspecified. For a named experimental condition or author-defined construct, check its operational definition and surrounding table or paragraph before classifying the label. If the operation is correct but the shorthand may mislead, report a naming clarification and consider a local gloss; do not call it a scientific error merely because the label is abbreviated. Professional wording need not be elaborate. An “AI feel” is an editing concern, not an authorship finding.

This library defaults to no explanatory colons or sentence-interrupting dashes in generated prose, including spaced hyphens and LaTeX dash syntax. Preserve compound-word hyphens, notation, minus signs, ranges, ratios, URLs, citations, exact quotations, and required templates. Author or venue instructions take precedence. Do not replace paragraphs with bullet fragments, mechanically erase punctuation, or impose a sentence-length quota. Apply these checks only within the requested task; a source or submission check does not itself authorize prose edits.

## Check the two deliverables

Audit coverage, evidence scope, work-status language, and word/page constraints. For paired edits, check that the letter's stated changes exist in the intended manuscript version and that quoted text and locations match it. Source checks, compilation, render inspection, and submission are different completion states.

Return only the requested deliverable plus material unresolved dependencies. A supported no-change decision is valid. Stop when the in-scope concerns have honest dispositions; do not turn a narrow reply into a whole-paper rewrite or claim that reviewer satisfaction or acceptance is guaranteed.
