# Formats and response patterns

Use the applicable format below. All examples are fictional; they illustrate decisions, not phrases that must be copied.

## A compact rebuttal

Allocate space by the effect of each concern on the contribution's credibility. Combine shared concerns when permitted, using headings that tell the reader what is answered: “Comparator definition and evidence,” “Sensitivity to annotations,” or “Scope of the theoretical interpretation.” Keep a private coverage map so minor requests do not disappear into a final “implementation details” paragraph.

An effective local sequence is **answer → evidence → interpretation/boundary → action**. Omit a component only when it adds nothing or is inapplicable. Present a compact table when several responses rely on the same results; define its population, endpoint, denominator, and uncertainty once. Cross-reference it rather than repeating every number. Follow separate-reviewer visibility rules if they apply.

**Example: composite comparison.** A method fits six candidates and selects one; the baseline fits one predetermined configuration. Both use the same update budget per fit. In a new stress test, success counts are 49/80 and 34/80. These facts support an observed advantage for the selected procedure in that test. They do not establish equal total training cost, statistical significance without the appropriate analysis, or the isolated effect of candidate selection.

If an original evaluation also exists, label the two studies separately. Do not pool their counts or use a stress-test interval as the original benchmark's significance test.

The opening can briefly identify the most consequential clarification or verified new evidence. Avoid spending scarce space listing praise or the volume of work. Additional experiments matter when they answer the concern.

## A point-by-point revision letter

Preserve stable comment IDs and quote or faithfully summarize each request. Split a compound comment if it asks different questions. A useful response unit is:

> **Comment [ID / faithful summary]**
>
> **Response**
>
> [Direct answer, evidence, and any remaining boundary.]
>
> **Change and location**
>
> [Actual completed action and verified section/table/page/lines, or an explicit pending status.]
>
> **Revised text, when useful**
>
> [Exact checked quotation from the intended version.]

The template is optional. Follow the requested format and avoid repeating a lengthy passage when a precise location suffices. If pagination is not final, use a verified section or mark the page/line field pending. Once the final render exists, reconcile its locations with the letter.

Do not describe proposed replacement text as already inserted. If the task authorizes both files, make the bounded edits and then verify the corresponding response statements. A draft-only task returns proposed text clearly labeled as such. A cover letter, when requested, summarizes major changes and unresolved points without replacing the point-by-point response.

## Recurring difficult cases

| Situation | Useful response | Inference to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reviewer missed material already in the paper | Answer directly, locate the existing material, and assess whether it needs a clearer introduction. | “Already stated” as a substitute for explanation, or inventing a new revision. |
| Multiple reviewers ask the same question | Give one coherent answer with a coverage map, if the format permits grouping. | Treating a shared answer as permission to omit a reviewer's distinct subquestion. |
| Agreement between annotators is high | Report the annotation task, rubric, sample, and agreement; identify any untested noise intervention. | Claiming robustness to corrupted labels without a relevant perturbation study. |
| A perturbation estimate has a wide interval spanning zero | State the observed estimate and uncertainty in the tested setting. | Equivalence, no effect, or universal robustness. |
| Validation candidates rank closely | Explain what the selection statistic measures and how uncertain the separation is. | Claiming calibrated downstream performance or proof of the unique best candidate. |
| Theory analogy is too strong | Describe the actual quantity/objective and restrict the theoretical interpretation. | Importing guarantees from a similarly shaped formula. |
| Reviewer asks for an unperformed experiment | State the available evidence and remaining gap; narrow a claim or give an authorized plan. | Reporting the experiment as complete or promising work on the author's behalf. |
| Reviewer requests a missing numerical threshold | Recover the actual historical definition or explicitly mark it unresolved. | Inventing a threshold from a current default to make the reply look complete. |
| Two reviews recommend incompatible changes | Identify the common scientific issue, explain the supported choice, and address both requests. | Silently choosing one, attacking the other, or treating every preference as mandatory. |

Evidence can support a confident answer within its scope. Replacing every conclusion with “may” or “possibly” does not repair an unsupported inference. Correct the inference itself.
