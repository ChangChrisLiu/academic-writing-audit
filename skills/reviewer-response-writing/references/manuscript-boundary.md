# The manuscript boundary

The response records a conversation and a revision. The manuscript teaches a reader who has not seen that conversation. Use the same evidence, but write for these different purposes.

## Paired examples

These examples are fictional. Completion claims below apply only under the explicitly supplied status.

### 1. Clarify an operation

**Supplied status:** The current Methods section has been edited and checked to contain the sentence shown below. Final page numbers are unavailable.

**Response:** “The comparator uses a predetermined linear score with fixed group multipliers and fits one model. We have added this operational definition to Methods, under ‘Comparators.’”

**Manuscript:** “The comparator uses a predetermined linear score with fixed group multipliers and fits one model.”

The response identifies the repair. The paper supplies the definition. Neither invents page numbers or says that the reviewer misunderstood.

### 2. Narrow attribution without deleting the finding

**Supplied evidence:** A selected system succeeds in 49/80 stress-test cases; a fixed comparator succeeds in 34/80. Selection opportunity and weighting both differ. No manuscript edit has been made.

**Draft response:** “The stress test shows a higher observed success rate for the selected procedure. Because weighting and selection opportunity differ together, this comparison does not isolate selection's contribution. We propose replacing the attribution sentence with the following text.”

**Proposed manuscript text:** “The selected procedure succeeded in 49/80 stress-test cases, compared with 34/80 for the fixed comparator. This comparison evaluates the combined procedure; it does not isolate the contribution of candidate selection.”

The finding remains. The causal attribution changes. Labeling the text as proposed avoids implying that a source edit has already happened.

### 3. A requested experiment remains unavailable

**Supplied evidence:** Two annotators agree on 94 of 100 labels. No training run with corrupted labels has been performed. The author has not committed to one.

**Response:** “The agreement study measures annotation consistency. It does not test the model's sensitivity to corrupted training labels, which remains unmeasured. We therefore cannot claim robustness to that intervention.”

**Manuscript, where this boundary matters:** “Annotators agreed on 94 of 100 labels under the specified rubric. Sensitivity to corrupted training labels was not evaluated.”

Do not add a promised experiment. Do not scatter the same limitation across every section; place it with the claim whose interpretation it limits.

### 4. Presentation is still a manuscript problem

**Situation:** The response clearly explains a method, but the paper still opens with undefined symbols and sends readers to a general appendix pointer.

**Repair:** Extract the plain operation from the response and integrate it before the first formal definition. Preserve the method's actual order and meaning; point to the appendix for concrete parameters. Then verify that the letter accurately describes the completed source change.

A clear reply does not prove that the revised manuscript is clear. Check the paper itself.

### 5. Missing input is not a study result

**Supplied evidence:** Group counts are available, but the author has not provided the uncertainty analysis. Its existence and outcome are unknown.

**Author note:** “Please provide or confirm the status of the uncertainty analysis before making an inferential claim.”

**Response and manuscript:** Report the supported counts descriptively, without declaring significance or asserting that no analysis exists. A paper sentence such as “No uncertainty analysis is available” would exceed this packet. If the author later confirms that no analysis was performed, that verified limitation can be stated where it matters.

Keep this distinction even when drafting from incomplete excerpts: access to a file, absence from an input packet, and the study's actual methods are different facts.

## Transfer test for an edited paragraph

Read the paragraph without the review thread. Can a new reader tell what was done, why it matters, and what the evidence establishes? If it requires knowing the objection or the revision history, recast that framing.

Remove conversational scaffolding such as “to address Reviewer 2,” “as requested,” “we have now clarified,” and “contrary to the concern.” Preserve any substantive definition, explanation, result, or limitation underneath it. This is a contextual edit, not a forbidden-word filter: a paper studying peer review can legitimately mention reviewers, and “not evaluated” can be an essential fact.

Prefer a bounded replacement or integration where the concept first becomes necessary. Do not append a defensive paragraph after an already adequate explanation. Do not remove useful interpretation just because it originated in a rebuttal, and do not move a central limitation to supplementary material merely to make the main text sound stronger.

For a final paired deliverable, reconcile the response against the actual manuscript version: changed wording, table and figure identities, reported numbers, quotation accuracy, and locations. Keep remaining author questions separate from submission-ready prose.
