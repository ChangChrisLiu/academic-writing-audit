# Research Manuscript Writing

Understand the study before writing its story. Produce concise, connected prose in which each section advances the research question and each claim has an identifiable basis.

## Establish the study before drafting

Read the current manuscript or research packet, relevant source material, and the author's requested scope. Determine the research direction, concrete problem, audience, study design or form of argument, contribution, strongest supported finding, and what that finding cannot establish. Distinguish an audit, a draft, and an authorized file edit. Preserve sound passages and the author's terminology.

Form a short internal account of why the problem matters, what existing work enables, what remains unresolved, why the proposed approach addresses that need, and what the study actually establishes. This is a reasoning aid, not a checklist to paste into the manuscript. A title, method name, or collection of fashionable keywords is not enough to supply this account.

If a missing fact changes the scientific story, ask a focused question or identify the exact dependency. Continue the parts supported by the evidence. Do not fill the gap with a generic field overview, invented motivation, claimed novelty, fabricated citation, or plausible result. Do not demand a questionnaire when the needed facts are already supplied. Verify literature claims against the permitted sources; a source's silence means unknown, not absent.

## Give each section a distinct job

The default abstract moves from concrete task need through the existing route's limitation and specific unresolved difficulty to the proposed approach and principal evidence. Explain the contribution at the level of the work, not as a list of implementation modules. Include a bounded implication only when it adds meaning. Preserve this causal progression without imposing a fixed sentence count or a universal structured-abstract template.

The introduction narrows from a relevant larger direction to what that direction has achieved, the specific gap, the reason the proposed approach is motivated by that gap, and the paper's supported contribution. Broad context should lead directly to the study's question. Motivation explains why the proposed scientific move makes sense; it is not another declaration that a gap exists. Contributions should follow from this reasoning in prose, not become a list by default.

Derive two or three connected literature threads from the introduction's argument. Explain what existing studies investigated, how they worked, what they established, and how those studies connect. Avoid a taxonomy of separate camps, one paragraph per paper, and repeated comparisons with the present manuscript. Meaningful thread headings are allowed; they do not require mutually exclusive categories. Put the present paper's positioning in the introduction or discussion. Use a different thread count or explicit comparison only when the author, article type, or required format calls for it, without inventing sources to fill a quota.

Explain prior contributions in their own studied settings. Do not organize paragraph endings around what those studies did not establish for this paper's task; that is still a current-paper comparison even without the word our. Include a limitation when it is needed to interpret the prior finding itself, rather than to advertise the present study's gap. Put unresolved source-coverage notes outside the manuscript passage when they do not affect its scientific meaning.

Methods follow the dependencies needed to understand the actual procedure or argument. Introduce the objects and purpose, then explain each operation, its inputs and outputs, and the next step it enables. Define a concept where it becomes needed. An overview, equation, and algorithm can have different reader functions; avoid narrating all three verbatim or repeating assurances about rigor. Keep decisive assumptions and conditions visible and use an appendix for actual detail, not as a substitute for explanation.

Read [section reasoning](references/section-reasoning.md) for abstract, introduction, literature, or method work. Read [findings and endings](references/findings-and-endings.md) for Results, Discussion, Limitations, or Conclusion. Choose their structure from the discipline and study design rather than forcing every paper into a benchmark narrative.

## Keep the prose focused

Ask what concrete question each paragraph answers and why it follows the preceding paragraph. If a sentence could fit an unrelated paper unchanged, inspect whether it supplies necessary context or is empty framing. Remove claims of importance that have no specific consequence, empty transitions, repeated contribution announcements, and defensive assurances. Do not delete an essential premise merely because it is general.

Write connected paragraphs with concrete subjects and manageable clauses. Preserve technical terms, scientific scope, uncertainty, and distinctions. Check concepts before choosing simpler words; an unfamiliar term or defined study label is not automatically wrong. Neither the manuscript nor the editor's explanation may add an unreported property or mechanism. Leave already accurate prose intact.

Generated exposition defaults to no explanatory colons or sentence-interrupting dashes. Preserve compound hyphens, notation, minus signs, ranges, ratios, URLs, exact quotations, and required templates. Author and venue instructions take precedence. Concision means removing repeated meaning while retaining the logical bridge, not enforcing a sentence quota or converting prose into bullets. Lists remain appropriate for a requested checklist, formal algorithm, or required reporting format.

Develop the reasoning rather than merely placing correct facts in order. Make clear why a supported difficulty motivates a choice and how the evidence changes or limits the interpretation. Connect paragraphs through the question left open or the next necessary step. Do not invent the authors' initial beliefs, failed attempts, surprise, or design history. Give consequential distinctions more room than routine setup; vary syntax and paragraph shape when their functions differ, while preserving useful parallelism and stable terms. Do not force identical paragraph arcs, decorative contrasts, or sentence-length patterns to appear more human.

## Check the revision against its purpose

When revising, compare the supplied earlier and new passages. Check whether the intended scientific question is better answered and whether valid premises, conditions, definitions, evidence links, and interpretation survive. Match this judgment to the discipline and design, separating a reviewer's validity concern from an editor's expression choice. Do not restore corrected errors or useful deletions; repair only actual losses. If no earlier version is available, limit the claim to the current draft rather than asserting preservation.

## Deliver in the requested medium

Read [LaTeX and Word delivery](references/latex-and-word.md) for a formatted draft or source edit. Maintain the existing template, citation system, and cross-references. Prose quality and successful document rendering are separate checks. Never claim a compiled PDF or validated Word layout from text inspection alone.

Return the requested section or document, with only material unresolved facts and brief explanations of substantive changes. Keep reviewer-response status and negotiation language out of manuscript exposition. Before finishing, trace the question through motivation, contribution, evidence, and conclusion, then check that each section performs its own job without replaying the whole story.
