# From research question to connected sections

## Abstract

For an ordinary first-draft abstract, use at most six sentences by default. This is a ceiling, not six compulsory slots or a reason to pad a shorter complete account. Follow explicit author instructions and required venue formats when they differ. Count the actual sentences after drafting, recognizing abbreviations, citations and decimal points; do not meet the ceiling by joining an inventory with semicolons or overloading clauses.

Plan the reasoning before the wording. Start with the particular task need, show how the available route leaves a consequential difficulty, and explain why the study's scientific idea addresses it. Keep the research question, contribution, and implementation mechanism at their proper levels. A weighting rule, filter, or selection step may implement the contribution without being the whole problem the work addresses. Preserve the premise that makes each next sentence follow; a transition word cannot supply it. Method detail belongs here only when it explains that central idea.

Close with the principal supported result and the scope needed to interpret it. For an empirical result, keep the measured endpoint, relevant comparison and evaluation setting aligned; select the decisive finding rather than enumerate every experiment. Do not append a generic significance slogan, reassurance or future-work sentence after the result. If compression loses motivation or changes the endpoint, remove secondary detail or repeated meaning instead of erasing the reasoning or enlarging the claim.

For example, a fictional crop study concerns detecting short irrigation interruptions. Sparse routine measurements miss the transitions; event-triggered readings may capture them, but readings collected at different times require alignment. A timing-aware estimator aligns the records, and the supplied experiment reports its error under the tested protocol. Those relationships give an abstract its logic. A catalogue of sensors, filters, datasets, and metrics does not.

This progression is a default for a problem-driven research abstract. A required structured abstract retains its mandated headings and content. A theoretical abstract ends in its supported theorem or conclusion under the necessary assumptions; an interpretive abstract ends in its supported interpretation of the stated corpus or setting. If results are not yet available, return a clearly provisional rationale or outline and identify that dependency outside the prose. Do not fabricate a result to complete the arc. Do not copy an exemplar's task, claims, opening words or exact sentence-to-function allocation.

## Introduction

For a first draft, the introduction's story follows the investigation rather than constraining it. Revisit the author's initial gap against relevant recent work, foundations and counterevidence before choosing the final paragraph sequence. Read [literature investigation and story development](literature-to-story.md) when that groundwork is missing. A literature review may change the question or contribution; it is not a search for citations to decorate an already fixed pitch.

Set the destination before choosing the emphasis. Establish conference or journal, track or article type, intended readers, and the question or contribution that the supplied venue guidance prioritizes. Keep an author preference, an official requirement, and an inferred reader need distinct. Do not assume that all conferences prize a new method or that every journal demands a mechanism. When the target would change the framing but is unknown, ask for it and develop only the supported reasoning until it is resolved, unless a venue-neutral draft was explicitly requested.

Before composing the opening, choose the perspective that gives these readers the shortest supported route to the actual research question. Identify the concrete problem the opening must lead to and what the reader needs to understand for that move. A task need, observed phenomenon or established capability can provide that entry; do not default to a grand field overview or present a menu of openings unless requested. Preserve an author's sound opening rather than replacing it with a stock template.

Develop an inverted-pyramid argument, not merely decreasing topic breadth. Show what relevant progress makes possible, the condition under which a specific difficulty remains, why that difficulty matters, and how the proposed scientific move responds. Then state the supported contribution and evidence. These are connected reasoning functions, not required headings, one paragraph per function, or slots to fill with invented facts.

For a conference introduction, integrate the gap and motivation economically. Readers still need to understand what is missing and why the contribution follows, even without sentences announcing “the gap” or “our motivation.” For a journal introduction, state the unresolved issue and its consequence explicitly, then show why the proposed approach addresses it. Greater explicitness does not mean a longer opening, repeated rationale, or a tutorial. The actual venue and article requirements take precedence over this default distinction.

Every transition needs a premise. Existing tools can already perform X; under condition Y they leave question Z unresolved; Z matters because of consequence W; the proposed operation addresses Z through a stated relationship. This reasoning must come from the study. Do not invent a limitation of prior work to make the funnel look complete.

Before writing polished prose, map the chosen opening to the problem and the paragraph sequence in a brief internal account. Each paragraph should develop the question opened by the previous one and leave the next step ready. Explain a competing route through what it enables, the requirements relevant here, and how those requirements bear on this study's question. Keep each requirement attached to the route it actually describes; do not turn a constraint on one approach into a limitation of all learning or research. Merge overlapping obstacles and express their consequence rather than listing disadvantages to dismiss the alternative. A shared keyword or connector does not connect unrelated topics. Once the argument has narrowed, remove detours instead of reopening a survey or hiding the jump behind “however.”

For every doubtful sentence, identify what it advances in this argument. Remove or relocate background exposition, implementation detail, repeated justification, and anticipated-reviewer explanations that do not help establish the gap, motivation, contribution, or its warranted scope here. An explanation can advance the story when it supplies the premise, distinction, or brief definition needed for the next inference. Read across a proposed deletion; if the contribution becomes an unexplained announcement, retain the smallest necessary bridge. Do not require a new claim in every sentence or mistake all explanation for defensive writing.

The opening should be only as broad as needed. Generic claims that a field is rapidly developing or important add little without a concrete consequence. Separate a literature gap, a practical obstacle, and an untested hypothesis. A small reviewed source set cannot establish that no one has studied an issue. State supplied scope honestly and leave unsupported priority unresolved.

Close with contributions as connected claims at the paper's scientific level. Once the motivation has established why the proposed operation follows, let the contribution paragraph add what the study did and found. Do not replay the same source roles, integration rationale, or tested scope merely to make that paragraph self-contained within an already continuous introduction. Keep a restatement only when it performs a distinct job, such as qualifying a broader claim. A coefficient or software utility is not automatically a separate scientific contribution. A contribution list is a format choice only when requested or required. Preserve an already coherent motivation instead of replacing it with the same template.

## Literature review

Continue the introduction's question through a source-supported account of what the field has learned and what that makes the reader able to understand next. Do not begin by choosing a number of directions or assigning papers to bins. For example, a question about observing short events can connect how observations are obtained to how their timing affects interpretation. Select and order studies because that relationship matters, not because a literature section needs several categories.

Let each paragraph establish a scientific point through the relevant operations and findings, then connect to the next point. Synthesize sources that support the same proposition. Check the factual relationship expressed by a transition. A shared topic does not prove that one study extends another, that all named works use the same mechanism, or that the field follows one historical lineage. Keep differences in data, update rules, systems and evaluation objects explicit when merging them would make the sentence false. Neither one paragraph per paper nor a concealed list of method families supplies a story.

The supplied source set is not a checklist that every paragraph must exhaust. Include studies that establish, qualify or challenge a necessary scientific point. When the only connection is a broad topic, narrow the passage or omit the tangential source rather than invent a tighter link. Retain consequential counterevidence; selecting a coherent line must not select only favorable findings. Explain material scope choices outside the passage when needed.

Use connected prose as the default. Headings may orient a long review or satisfy a required format, but the argument must still progress across them; a descriptive heading is not a reason to partition the field. If no introduction is supplied, use the supported research question provisionally and state the missing context outside the passage. Explicit novelty auditing or author-requested current-paper comparisons are separate modes, not defaults for the literature narrative.

Explain prior contributions in their own studied settings. Do not organize paragraph endings around what those studies did not establish for this paper's task; that is still a current-paper comparison even without the word our. Include a limitation when it is needed to interpret the prior finding itself, rather than to advertise the present study's gap. Put unresolved source-coverage notes outside the manuscript passage when they do not affect its scientific meaning.

Use the citation keys or bibliographic information actually provided. Put citations at the claim they support. Do not turn a source card into evidence that an entire paper was read. Identify material source gaps outside the prose rather than padding the review with generic sentences.

## Method

Give the reader the minimum map needed to follow the process, then move in dependency order. Define data or materials, relevant assumptions and objects, transformations or analytical steps, and the outputs used downstream. In theoretical work the dependency may run from definitions and assumptions through lemmas to a proof; qualitative methods may follow access, sampling, materials, analysis, and researcher position. Use what the study actually did.

A sentence explains an operation when it says what is computed or interpreted, from what, and how it is used. An equation formalizes that operation; an algorithm may clarify execution order. Each representation should add understanding. Define stable concepts once in the connected narrative, then use them consistently. Concise recaps in a standalone figure or a distant section can still be useful.

Remove repeated assertions that the procedure is principled, robust, fair, or carefully designed. Retain concrete safeguards, eligibility conditions, failure paths, and limitations that affect the method. Keep critical definitions in the main account. Detailed constants, derivations, or protocols can go to an accessible appendix only when the main explanation remains interpretable and reproducibility is preserved.
