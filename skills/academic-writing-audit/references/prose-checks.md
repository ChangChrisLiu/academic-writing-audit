# Connected and concise prose

Use these checks after reconstructing the scientific argument. They apply to the requested passage, not as a reason to restyle an otherwise sound manuscript. The punctuation choice is this library's configurable prose preference, not a universal academic rule or an AI-authorship test.

## Reasoning at the sentence boundary

Identify what the first sentence establishes and what the next sentence adds. Track the same subject, study, endpoint, and conditions across the transition. A causal connector needs causal support. A contrast needs a real difference. An ambiguous “this” needs a recoverable referent. Correct an unsupported proposition or identify the missing premise instead of hiding it behind a softer verb.

Check the bridge to a new section or paragraph. If removing a seemingly general sentence leaves a method without motivation or a result without its question, retain or clarify the bridge. A full audit also checks agreement between the abstract, main text, captions, and conclusion.

## Word choice and sentence usage

Check whether each consequential term names the intended concept and whether the word combination is natural in the field. Use supplied definitions and evidence before replacing an unfamiliar expression. Keep valid specialist terminology and clearly defined new concepts. Replace an inflated synonym or vague invented label with the concrete operation it is meant to describe. Flag unresolved meaning instead of inventing a polished definition.

Check grammar, prepositions, modifiers, pronouns, and who performs the action. A sentence can be short but still assign a procedure to the wrong subject or use an ambiguous label. Do not make it sound more professional by stacking nouns or adding rare vocabulary. Maintain one term per concept without conflating distinct quantities, and keep evidential force intact when replacing words such as significant or validated. Distinguish an incorrect term, unclear expression, and optional preference in the findings.

## Sentence load

Find the subject and main verb. Inspect intervening clauses, nested qualifications, stacked abstract nouns, and several claims compressed into one sentence. Separate independent assertions when that reduces the reader's memory burden. Keep assumptions, exclusions, and uncertainty with the statements they limit.

Do not replace all long sentences with short ones or apply a fixed word threshold. A clear theorem may require several conditions in one sentence. A sequence of short fragments may be harder to follow than one connected explanation. Preserve field terminology rather than rotating synonyms for variety.

## Repeated meaning

Compare what each sentence tells the reader. Merge or delete repetitions that perform the same function nearby. An introductory promise followed by the same promise in different words adds little. A Results paragraph should interpret the comparison rather than narrate each table cell.

Retain repetition with a distinct purpose. A standalone caption may need the endpoint and denominator already defined in Methods. An abstract may restate the main finding. A later application of a definition is not redundant merely because the same term appears again.

## Natural expression

Remove empty importance claims, repeated reassurance, canned transitions, and forced parallel lists when they replace concrete information. Preserve necessary contrast and supported interpretation. No individual word proves AI authorship, and avoiding certain words does not establish better reasoning.

For generated prose, use connected paragraphs without explanatory colons or sentence-interrupting dashes under the library's default. Preserve compound-word hyphens and technical or quoted content. Rewrite the grammar instead of blindly deleting punctuation or replacing every dash with a semicolon. Honor the author's format or venue requirements.

## Final read

Read the repaired passage as a first-time reader. Check that each sentence has a clear job, the inference remains supported, and important conditions survived. Compare quantities, citations, assumptions, negation, and scope with the source. Explain substantive corrections separately from optional style choices. Stop once the named problems are resolved.
