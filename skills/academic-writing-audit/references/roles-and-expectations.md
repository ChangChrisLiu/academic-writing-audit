# Roles, expertise, and the expected argument

Choose a reviewing lens from the actual scientific question and study design. A broad label such as medical reviewer or AI expert is insufficient if the decision concerns causal identification, a mathematical guarantee, or qualitative interpretation. Use the smallest set of lenses needed for the task.

## Bind the role to a decision

A useful role brief specifies the discipline or subfield, study design, intended readership, in-scope question, accessible evidence, applicable standards and their sources, and expected output. Infer what the packet establishes; ask only for decisive missing facts. Do not claim professional credentials or familiarity with unpublished conventions. If a standard is unknown, label that uncertainty or verify it when the task permits.

| Lens | Main responsibility | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scientific reviewer | Decide whether the stated question is answered by the argument and evidence, with appropriate scope. | Do not invent a stronger question, missing experiment, or reviewer's intent. |
| Methodological specialist | Check the specific inference or formal step on which a consequential claim depends. | Do not import unrelated disciplinary criteria or promise full verification without the necessary artifacts. |
| Scholarly editor | Improve progression, definitions, reader access, terminology, and expression while retaining valid content. | Fluency and brevity do not establish validity; word-count reduction cannot justify lost meaning. |
| First-time domain reader | Test whether the target readership can follow the objects, assumptions, and transitions. | Familiar specialist vocabulary need not be replaced for a general audience unless that audience is requested. |
| Revision checker | Compare before and after for concern resolution, semantic preservation, and new errors. | The old draft is not ground truth; preserve justified corrections and useful compression. |

These are responsibilities, not a requirement for five agents. One agent can use separate passes. Use an additional specialist only for an actual unresolved claim or authorized independent check. Match the design as well as the field. For example, a clinical observational association needs confounding and estimand scrutiny, a numerical theorem needs assumptions and quantifiers, and a reflexive interview study needs attention to the analytic stance and interpretation. These examples are not universal reporting rules.

## Reconstruct what the logic needs to accomplish

For each consequential concern, establish a short chain of **question → expected argument or evidence → current argument or evidence → gap → consequence → smallest action**. Include why that expectation applies. It may come from a stated claim, research question, actual reviewer comment, supplied reporting requirement, or an explicitly inferred reader need. Keep these sources distinct.

Ask whether the passage answers its question, not merely whether it contains relevant terms. A method section should make the operation and dependencies understandable. An experiment should connect the tested question to the comparison and permitted conclusion. A theoretical or interpretive section may answer through a proof or source-grounded interpretation instead. The correct standard depends on the retained claim; not every section needs a mechanism, ablation, significance test, or causal conclusion.

Distinguish missing explanation, unavailable evidence, inconsistent claims, and a concern already resolved in the current text. A paragraph can be valid yet fail to answer its stated question; an author response can be convincing while the paper remains unclear. Conversely, a reviewer can request evidence beyond the manuscript's claim. Explain the mismatch rather than obeying or dismissing the request by authority.

Where useful, challenge one pivotal inference with a plausible alternative explanation or counterexample. Use it to locate the needed premise or limit the claim. Do not invent observations, require exhaustive objections, or turn every local edit into a new study.

## Keep review and editing separable

First diagnose scientific validity and completeness of the argument. Then decide how to express or locate the necessary information. Do not silently convert an editor's style preference into a scientific defect. Do not let a reviewer demand repeated assurances when a direct definition resolves the concern.

For independent review, supply the current source, evidence, and role brief without the earlier verdicts when feasible. Independence means separate context and reasoning, not several names attached to one inherited conclusion. Reviewers should give source anchors, uncertainty, and keep decisions. Consolidate disagreements by checking the disputed proposition against the evidence and reader need. Preserve material disagreements when the available evidence cannot decide them; do not vote or average recommendations.

## Check the result

Revisit the expectation after the proposed repair. Has the reader's scientific question actually been answered, and did the edit preserve the conditions, motivation, endpoint, and interpretation that made the original claim intelligible? A narrowed claim may be the correct resolution. A still-missing experiment may remain an honest dependency. Keep response status and reviewer-directed language outside manuscript exposition.

Report a supported disposition rather than simulated acceptance. A role-based review does not prove expert validation, reviewer satisfaction, or improved model performance. Stop when the requested judgments are supported, leaving sound prose intact.
