# Academic Writing Audit

Make the research argument easier to follow while preserving its evidential meaning and the author's voice. Remove unnecessary self-defense; retain the conditions needed to understand the result.

## Establish the task

Identify the current source, requested scope, and mode: **audit**, **targeted revision**, or **final check**. An audit request returns findings before rewriting. An explicit revision request authorizes the bounded edit; do not add an approval gate. Honor narrower instructions.

Read the passage in context and form an independent account before consulting earlier judgments. Separate current source facts, author decisions, inspected evidence, and unresolved assertions. Prior reviews locate concerns, not current truth. An excerpt supports only an excerpt-level verdict.

## Choose expertise and define the expected logic

For a scientific or reviewer-informed audit, read [roles and expectations](references/roles-and-expectations.md). Match the lens to the discipline, design, audience, and judgment. Separate scientific validity from expression; additional specialists or agents are optional.

For each consequential issue, establish **question → required support and why → current support → remaining gap**. Distinguish actual review requests from inferred reader needs. Do not demand more than the retained claim requires or accept a topic mention or confident reply as resolution.

Apply the same evidence standard to favorable and unfavorable judgments at the study's actual stage. Locate the affected claim and distinguish contradiction, unresolved question, and scope limitation. Preserve supported contributions without demanding universal certainty. Keep exploratory debate in the research record and consequential limitations in the manuscript.

## Review in this order

1. **Argument:** Reconstruct problem → gap → contribution → reasoning → evidence → conclusion. Preserve complementary roles and check transitions between objects or stages. Read [argument and evidence](references/argument-and-evidence.md) for a scientific logic, claim, or comparator audit.
2. **Evidence:** Bind claims to their source, actual comparison, endpoint, and permitted inference. Separate observation, interpretation, attribution, and generalization; keep studies and versions distinct.
3. **Reader function:** Judge the fact's importance, formulation, and placement separately. Read [nondefensive writing](references/nondefensive-writing.md) for defensive tone or shortening decisions.
4. **Revision:** Repair a named problem with the smallest change. Preserve coherent passages verbatim when no defect exists. Broad motivation may supply the essential bridge to the contribution.
5. **Prose:** Check sentence connections, referents, clause complexity, and repeated meaning without strengthening claims. Read [prose checks](references/prose-checks.md) for a whole-passage clarity pass.
6. **Verification:** Re-read the connected passage and check the diff against evidence. Read [review and patching](references/review-and-patching.md) for file edits, reviewer-informed revision, or final checks, and [worked examples](references/examples.md) for ambiguous keep/delete decisions.

## Check the manuscript's progression

Check whether a concrete question motivates the method. An ordinary abstract normally has at most six sentences and connects task need, difficulty and contribution before closing with the principal supported result and its necessary scope. Preserve author or required venue formats; do not force six slots, overload sentences or invent missing results. The introduction's chosen opening should lead through relevant progress, gap and motivation to contribution. Generic importance statements and module lists cannot supply these links. Preserve coherent passages.

For an investigated first-draft story, check its closest prior solution and meaningful counterevidence in the existing record. Separate established progress, documented limitations, plausible questions, and access-related unknowns. Introductory gap assertions and agent agreement do not verify a field-wide gap. Request only consequential missing checks, not a new survey for every edit.

For an apparently obvious gap, check prior attempts, constraints, and alternative terminology. Prior failure is an investigation priority, not a verdict. Separate observed outcomes from inferred causes or universal impossibility. A renewed attempt may rest on an evidenced change, a hypothesis, or a useful replication question. Unknown causes remain unknown and research-planning debate stays outside manuscript prose.

Before judging introduction emphasis, establish the venue, track or article type, readers, and evidenced priorities. Separate supplied criteria from inference; leave venue-specific judgments unresolved when the target is missing. Trace one narrowing line across paragraphs. Conference framing may integrate gap and motivation compactly; journal framing should make the unresolved issue, consequence, and rationale explicit. Actual requirements govern. Remove detours while retaining the smallest premise or definition needed for the next inference, and recheck after shortening.

Literature should develop the introduction's question through connected scientific points, with no preset number of directions. Explain what studies established and why the next point follows, rather than listing method families or adding repeated present-paper contrasts. Check that shared predicates and claims of extension or dependence are supported; a smooth story must not merge unlike mechanisms into a false lineage. Put present-paper positioning in the introduction or discussion unless explicitly requested otherwise. Methods follow dependency order; findings and endings follow the design and supplied conventions.

## Make the decision explicit

| Decision | Apply when |
| --- | --- |
| Keep | The sentence performs a necessary, distinct job and reads clearly. |
| Clarify | A definition, bridge, or inference is missing or misleading. |
| Merge | Two passages repeat the same fact for the same reader purpose. |
| Move | The fact belongs elsewhere; the destination can carry it without hiding an essential condition. |
| Delete | The passage adds reassurance, rebuttal staging, or repetition without a necessary reader function. |

Necessary negation, eligibility rules, uncertainty, unequal budgets, and genuine limitations are not defects in tone. Conversely, an important fact does not justify every wording or every repetition. Explain an operation before referring to its parameter appendix.

## Prose when drafting or revising

Read the [prose policy](references/prose-policy.md) when drafting or revising manuscript prose or audit explanations. Preserve scientific meaning, valid terminology, necessary conditions, and sound author choices. Resolve labels against operational definitions, and do not invent a mechanism or property in either the manuscript or the diagnosis. A writing preference does not authorize out-of-scope edits.

## Deliver a bounded result

For an audit, provide a short overall judgment, prioritized findings with locations and evidence, explicit keep decisions, and material unresolved facts. Distinguish scientific errors, missing explanations, and optional style preferences.

For a revision, provide the replacement or patch, why it is needed, and what was preserved. Flag a substantive evidence conflict instead of inventing a reconciliation. Report source inspection, compilation, rendering, and external publication separately, and only when observed.

Stop when the requested problems are resolved and the argument remains intact. No justified change is a successful outcome. Do not expand a writing audit into new experiments, exhaustive paperwork, or a wholesale rewrite. These are reasoning roles for one agent; extra agents, models, and services are optional within authorized scope.
