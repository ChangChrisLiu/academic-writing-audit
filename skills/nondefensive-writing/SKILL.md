# Nondefensive Writing

Turn anticipated objections into useful information for the reader. Remove the performance of reassurance while preserving the definitions, boundaries, and interpretation that make a statement accurate.

## Establish the passage's job

Read the surrounding sentences and the supplied evidence. Determine the requested scope and whether the user wants diagnosis or replacement text. An authorized revision can proceed directly. A manuscript paragraph and a response letter have different purposes: a response letter may address a reviewer explicitly; the paper should explain the research.

For each questionable sentence, assess three things separately:

1. **Function:** What would readers misunderstand if the underlying fact disappeared?
2. **Formulation:** Does the wording convey that fact, or stage an objection and an assurance?
3. **Placement:** Does the reader need it here, and is the same job already done nearby?

An important fact can have poor wording or excessive repetition. Conversely, negative wording can carry an essential rule. Do not classify a sentence by words such as “not,” “only,” “fixed,” “excluded,” or “limited.”

In an introduction, test each doubtful sentence against the narrowing line from existing progress through gap and motivation to contribution. Remove explanation that merely repeats background or anticipates an objection without advancing that line. Keep the short premise, definition, or consequential boundary needed to understand the next step. A conference introduction may integrate gap and motivation compactly; a journal introduction should make their distinct reasoning explicit according to the target's requirements. Do not turn “avoid explanation for its own sake” into deletion of the rationale that makes the work necessary.

## Choose a concrete edit

- **Keep** a clear definition, algorithmic exclusion, study endpoint, assumption, consequential limitation, or supported interpretation.
- **Clarify** a useful fact hidden behind “we stress,” “it should be emphasized,” or a claim of rigor. State the actual operation or observation.
- **Merge** assurances that repeat the same protocol fact for the same purpose.
- **Move** detail only when its new location still supports the relevant inference. Do not hide a consequential limitation in an appendix to improve tone.
- **Delete** rebuttal staging or repetition that adds no necessary reader information.

Preserve differences in data, tuning, eligibility, or resources when they change the interpretation of a comparison. Preserve what was not measured when readers might otherwise infer that endpoint. A precise boundary can replace several generic disclaimers, but brevity does not justify concealing it.

Keep useful Results interpretation. “Consistent with the proposed explanation” may be appropriate when supported; merely adding “may” cannot rescue an explanation with no evidential link. State the observation and identify the missing link instead. Do not convert descriptive findings into causal or safety claims.

## Worked example

**Draft:** “We emphasize that incomplete measurements were not quietly discarded. They were not assigned a concentration estimate. No one should infer that our system guarantees reliable field operation.”

**Supplied facts:** Incomplete measurements receive an abstention, remain in the denominator, and have no concentration estimate. Field operation was not evaluated.

**Repair:** “Incomplete measurements received an abstention rather than a concentration estimate and remained in the evaluation denominator. The study did not evaluate field operation.”

The repair removes accusation and reassurance while retaining two necessary negative facts. If the preceding sentence already explains the measurement challenge clearly, leave it unchanged.

## Prose when drafting or revising

Use connected paragraphs with concrete subjects and verbs. Remove empty staging and repeated assurances. Check the inference and referents between adjacent sentences. Simplify nested clauses while keeping conditions attached to their claims. Merge repeated propositions serving the same reader purpose, but retain necessary definitions, limitations, and standalone summaries. Keep technical terms stable. Resolve the intended concept, scope, and implied properties before changing a term. Separate a wrong concept from awkward expression of a correct one. Check word combinations, grammar, modifiers, and agency in context. Preserve valid specialist terms, defined new concepts, and disciplinary uses even when unfamiliar. For a language-only repair, retain the technical term. Keep a correct passage unchanged and flag uncertain meaning rather than guessing. Verify that replacements preserve units, conditions, distinctions, and evidential force; do not use global word bans or substitutions. Apply the same standard to the audit itself. Do not add an unreported mechanism, mathematical form, or property when paraphrasing the source or explaining a finding. Keep an unspecified operation unspecified. For a named experimental condition or author-defined construct, check its operational definition and surrounding table or paragraph before classifying the label. If the operation is correct but the shorthand may mislead, report a naming clarification and consider a local gloss; do not call it a scientific error merely because the label is abbreviated. Professional wording need not be elaborate. An “AI feel” is an editing concern, not an authorship finding.

This library defaults to no explanatory colons or sentence-interrupting dashes in generated prose, including spaced hyphens and LaTeX dash syntax. Preserve compound-word hyphens, notation, minus signs, ranges, ratios, URLs, citations, exact quotations, and required templates. Author or venue instructions take precedence. Do not replace paragraphs with bullet fragments, mechanically erase punctuation, or impose a sentence-length quota. Apply these checks only within the requested task; a source or submission check does not itself authorize prose edits.

## Deliver and stop

Return the requested audit or affected replacement text with brief reasons that identify what changed and what was preserved. Mark an unchanged passage explicitly when omission could be mistaken for deletion. Follow the user's requested format rather than imposing a long checklist.

Reread across each edit: the motivation-to-method bridge, endpoint, denominator, operative rule, and inference should retain their meaning wherever relevant. Stop once the defensive framing and actual redundancy are resolved. Do not continue polishing sound prose, add new caveats by habit, or turn an editorial request into an experimental program.
