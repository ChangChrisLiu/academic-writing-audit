# Editing examples

These fictional examples illustrate decisions. They are not a phrase bank or a measure of AI authorship. Apply the author's requested style while preserving evidence and established terminology.

## Empty staging and an unsupported mechanism

The draft reads “It is important to note that our approach offers a robust solution — it achieved 31/40 successes versus 26/40 for the baseline, which therefore proves that its weighting mechanism drives the improvement. This highlights the effectiveness of our approach.”

Only the full procedures were compared. A supported repair is “The full procedure achieved 31/40 successes, compared with 26/40 for the baseline. This comparison does not isolate the contribution of the weighting mechanism.”

The repair removes empty staging and a duplicate conclusion. The second sentence preserves the specific boundary needed to interpret the original mechanism claim. Do not repeat that boundary after every later mention of the result.

## A long sentence with a consequential condition

The draft reads “Samples, which were taken only after the temperature had stabilized and which included readings from sensors that had undergone calibration, were, provided that both channels were available, retained for the paired analysis, which was conducted at the participant level.”

The protocol confirms all four conditions. A repair is “Calibrated sensors recorded samples after the temperature stabilized. Samples entered the paired analysis only when both channels were available. The participant was the unit of analysis.”

Splitting without “only when both channels were available” would change eligibility. Replacing “participant” with “sample” would change the analysis. Fewer words do not compensate for either error.

## Sentence punctuation and scientific notation

The draft reads “The cross-domain model has one requirement: both channels must be present — otherwise it abstains.” A repair is “The cross-domain model requires both channels and abstains when either is missing.”

Keep “cross-domain” intact. Also retain technical strings such as `f: X → Y`, `a - b`, `1:2`, `10–15 °C`, `https://example.org`, and a verbatim quotation containing a colon or dash. Context decides whether punctuation is grammatical decoration or required content. Do not erase all matching characters.

## Repetition that serves different readers

An abstract reports “The intervention group completed 42/60 tasks.” The Results section gives the same count with the comparator, uncertainty, and analysis. A standalone caption identifies the endpoint and denominator.

These occurrences serve different reading contexts. Keep them when needed. Within one Results paragraph, a following sentence that merely says “The intervention group therefore completed 42 tasks out of 60” duplicates the same proposition and can be removed.

## Chinese prose with excessive staging

The draft reads “值得注意的是，本研究具有以下重要发现：参与者认为讨论很有帮助——这进一步充分说明了讨论的显著价值。换言之，讨论对参与者而言是有帮助的。”

The supplied evidence contains 12 interviews, 9 positive accounts, and 3 reports of added workload. No independent outcome measurement is supplied. A supported revision is “在 12 名受访者中，9 名认为讨论有帮助，3 名认为讨论增加了工作量。”

The revision retains the contrasting accounts and removes an unsupported effectiveness claim. It does not assert that no independent outcome measurement exists. That question belongs in an author note unless its absence is confirmed.

## A paragraph that should remain unchanged

“Routine measurements cover common conditions. Rare events are less often observed, leaving limited evidence for decisions in those conditions. We combine routine records with targeted follow-up measurements to address this coverage gap.”

With facts supporting this motivation and operation, keep the passage. The middle sentence supplies the reason for the method. Deleting it as a generic bridge would damage the argument.
